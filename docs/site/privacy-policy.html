Privacy Policy

C64 Commander for Android · effective 30 August 2026

The short version

This policy covers the C64 Commander app for Android.

C64 Commander has no accounts, no analytics, and no servers of its own. It does not collect, transmit, or share personal data. Everything it remembers, it remembers on your device, and the only machine it talks to without being asked is your own C64 Ultimate on your own network.

What is stored on your device

C64 Commander keeps a small amount of data locally, to do its job:

  • Saved devices: the name, address and ports of each C64 Ultimate you connect to.
  • Device passwords, where your machine asks for one. These are held in Android's secure storage.
  • Your settings and your library: appearance, playback preferences, playlists, disk collections, and any tunes you have marked as liked.
  • Logs and diagnostics, kept so you can see what the app did when something goes wrong.
  • Music you download, if you choose to install the High Voltage SID Collection.
  • A copy of your machine's KERNAL and BASIC ROMs, if you play SID music on this device. The app reads them from your own C64 Ultimate, because many tunes will not play without them. They stay on your device, are never uploaded, and are never included in a diagnostics report.

None of this is sent to the developer or to anyone else. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling it removes all of it.

What is never collected

  • Personal identifiers of any kind.
  • Analytics, usage metrics or behavioural tracking.
  • Automatic crash reporting.
  • Advertising identifiers.
  • Accounts, logins or profiles. There are none to create.

Every network request the app makes

This is the complete list.

  • Your C64 Ultimate. The app talks to the machine you configured, over your local network, to read its state, change its settings, transfer files and carry its sound and picture. This is the app's whole purpose and it happens continuously while connected.
  • The High Voltage SID Collection, only when you ask for it. The app downloads the archive from a public mirror, hvsc.brona.dk by default, and you can point it at another under Settings → HVSC. What comes back is music files and the archive's own documentation. Nothing about you is sent.
  • An online archive of disks and programs, only when you search it. The address is yours to set under Settings → Online Archive, and it defaults to commoserve.files.commodore.net. Your search terms go to whichever server you have configured.
  • Links you tap. Documentation and support links open in your browser and leave the app.

There is no other outbound traffic. The app requests no fonts, scripts or resources from third-party sites; it carries everything it draws.

Permissions

The app declares six Android permissions:

  • Internet, to reach your C64 Ultimate.
  • Multicast, to receive the picture and sound it sends out.
  • Foreground service and media playback, so music keeps playing when you leave the app.
  • Wake lock, so it keeps playing with the screen off.
  • Notifications, to show the playback notification while music plays in the background. This is the only one Android asks you to approve. It is requested the first time you start playback that carries on after you leave the app, and nothing else uses it: the app sends you no other notification. If you decline, playback still works, but you get no notification to tap your way back to the app from.

It asks for no access to your location, contacts, camera, microphone or photos, and it holds no storage permission. When you add your own files, Android's own file picker hands the app just the folder you chose.

Diagnostics you choose to share

The app can package its logs into a file for you to send on when you are reporting a problem. That only ever happens when you ask for it. The file holds recent activity, errors, your app version, your device model, and the name, address and firmware of the C64 Ultimate you are connected to.

Your device password is never in it. Its network address can be, so share the file with people you trust.

Platform telemetry

C64 Commander sends no crash reports or diagnostics anywhere. Android and Google Play may still collect crash information at the system level, according to the settings on your device and in Play, and that is outside the app's control.

Children

C64 Commander is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information about them.

Third-party software

The app is built on open-source libraries. The full list, with licences, ships with the source at THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md.

Changes
